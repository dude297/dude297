Ohmni

A schematic says 2.50 V. The meter reads 0.45 V. I built Ohmni to help explain that gap: confirm the circuit, calculate what it should do, and choose the next measurement that separates plausible faults.

Origin: BuilderBase Multi Model Hackathon · SF Tech Week · October 11, 2026. I built the prototype and continued developing the diagnosis and verification workflows after submission.

ROLEDeveloper
STATUSWorking prototype / active

01 / THE PROBLEM

The drawing and the measurement disagree.

For a 10 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider on a 5 V supply, the expected midpoint is 2.50 V. A measured 0.45 V is evidence to investigate. Ohmni tests alternate component values against the observation; in the recorded demo, a 1 kΩ lower resistor ranks as the first hypothesis.

The product connects tasks that usually live in separate places: reading the schematic, checking connectivity, solving the circuit, recording a meter reading, and deciding what to probe next.

02 / WHAT I BUILT

Model-assisted reading. Solver-owned calculations.

Extract a candidate graphGroq reads the schematic, with Gemini as a fallback. Structural validation checks the graph before it can reach the solver.
Make uncertainty reviewableThe user can correct component values and connections. The server requires a confirmed graph before solving it.
Compute voltages and currentsA local TypeScript implementation of modified nodal analysis calculates expected behavior. A language model does not supply the electrical answer.
Choose a useful next measurementThe probe guide re-solves candidate single faults and recommends a node whose voltage helps distinguish them. Repair verification is bound to the circuit revision.

I also added provider fallback behavior, request limits, server-side validation, and traces that show failures and skipped calls alongside successful ones.

03 / EVIDENCE & LIMITS

A diagnosis is a testable hypothesis.

The repository includes the divider demonstration, solver tests, synthetic schematic fixtures, and recorded UI flows. The hackathon diagnosis path covers DC resistive circuits; its single-fault ranking is relative to the faults it models. Real-photo extraction accuracy and time saved have not been measured.

The app rejects malformed or unsolvable graphs and can report that no modeled fault fits. Two models agreeing on an image does not remove the need to confirm its connections.
